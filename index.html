Research

Evidence-Grounded LLMs for Genetic Variant Interpretation

2026 – Present
Independent research, sole author · Manuscript "Where Does the Evidence Go?"
  • Built a benchmark of 13,500 ClinGen expert variant classifications, rebuilding each one's evidence as of its decision date from 38 archived ClinVar releases (6.2 GB), gnomAD, eight predictor scores and PubTator3 literature. The test set holds 3,781 variants approved after the models' release.
  • Designed a two-stage RAG system: an ACMG/AMP rule engine plus an LLM reader that cites a PMID and a quote for each evidence criterion. Quotes are verified against the source; invented quotes fell from 5.7% (7B) to 0.6% (32B).
  • Five open LLMs (7B–32B, fp16 and 4-bit NF4) scored below a rule engine and a logistic regression on the same evidence (0.520–0.571 vs. 0.664 and 0.751 balanced accuracy). Found two routes by which retrieved context leaks the experts' answer.
  • A placebo-controlled decomposition showed that claims shuffled across variants of the same expert panel recover most of the LLM readers' small gain, so that gain is largely not specific to the variant.

Evidence Reliance of LLMs in Clinical Risk Prediction

Oct 2026 – Present
Independent research, sole author · Manuscript "Which Way Do Open-Weight LLMs Follow…"
  • Built a vLLM log-probability harness and ran five sequential pre-registered studies on whether LLMs follow a clinical risk model's SHAP explanation differently when it points toward alarm or safety: over 1.3 million prompts, 17 open-weight checkpoints (4B–32B, eight families), three clinical datasets.
  • The registered alarm-deference hypothesis was refuted; a placebo explanation moved the models' answer prior toward "No" in 13 of 14 settings, so the measured direction depends on the baseline.
  • Found that larger Qwen3 models open their answers with Markdown bold, which silently breaks single-token probability reading; designed a two-step reading that recovers them at almost no cost by reusing the prefix cache.
  • Protocols tagged on GitHub before each study, SHA-256 checks on every run, and 248 automated checks of the paper's numbers.

SVBERT: Structural Variant Refinement with a Genomic Language Model

2025 – 2026
Undergraduate thesis, BRAC University (team of four)
  • Led the literature review and designed the pipeline for SVBERT, which refines long-read structural-variant calls by fusing LoRA-tuned DNABERT-2 embeddings with a CNN over strand-aware alignment features through cross-attention.
  • Highest deletion F1 among five methods on GIAB HG002 Nanopore (0.941) and PacBio CLR (0.938); best average F1 across five variant types on a simulated benchmark (0.88 vs. 0.83).
